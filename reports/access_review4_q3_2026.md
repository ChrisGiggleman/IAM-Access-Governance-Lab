**User:** James Taylor

## Observation

James Taylor is an active Sales Representative with directly assigned Manager-level access to the CRM system.

James already receives Standard CRM access through his normal role-based entitlement. The additional Manager-level access exceeds the standard permissions associated with the Sales Representative role.

The elevated access does not automatically indicate an access-control violation because James may have additional responsibilities requiring Manager-level CRM functions. However, the entitlement should be reviewed to confirm that the original business requirement still exists and that the elevated access remains appropriate.

## Investigation Steps

* Review the original access request, approval ticket, and supporting documentation for assignment A030.
* Determine the business justification for granting James Taylor Manager-level CRM access.
* Verify whether the elevated access was intended to be permanent or temporary.
* Review whether an expiration or end date was included in the original request.
* Confirm with Olivia Wilson that James still performs responsibilities requiring Manager-level CRM access.
* Identify which Manager-level functions James requires that are unavailable through his Standard CRM entitlement.
* Review the permissions associated with the CRM Manager access level.
* Determine whether James can view, modify, approve, reassign, or otherwise manage records beyond those required for his Sales Representative responsibilities.
* Compare the current entitlement against the principle of least privilege.
* Determine whether a lower level of access could support James's current responsibilities.
* Verify whether organizational policy requires elevated or temporary access to have an expiration date or periodic review.

## Risk Assessment

**Risk Level:** Medium

Manager-level CRM access may allow James Taylor to perform actions beyond the responsibilities of a standard Sales Representative.

Depending on the CRM permission model, elevated access may allow the user to view other representatives' records, modify customer or sales information, reassign records, approve actions, access broader reports, or perform other management-level functions.

If the elevated entitlement is no longer required, retaining the access creates a least-privilege concern and increases the potential impact of account misuse, credential compromise, or administrative error.

The risk should be evaluated based on the specific permissions provided by the Manager role, the documented business requirement, and the duration of the elevated access.

## Recommended Action

Do not immediately remove the Manager-level CRM entitlement solely because James Taylor's current role is Sales Representative.

Complete the access review and determine whether a current business justification exists.

If Manager-level access is still required:

1. Confirm the business justification with Olivia Wilson.
2. Verify that the elevated permissions are necessary for James's current responsibilities.
3. Confirm that Manager-level access is the minimum level required to perform those responsibilities.
4. Document the approving manager and supporting access request.
5. Apply an expiration date or future review date where appropriate.
6. Document the entitlement as an approved exception to the standard Sales Representative access model.

If the elevated access is no longer required or cannot be adequately justified:

1. Follow the established access-removal process.
2. Remove the directly assigned Manager-level CRM entitlement.
3. Verify that James retains his normal role-based Standard CRM access.
4. Confirm the resulting access aligns with his current Sales Representative responsibilities.
5. Document the remediation and final access state.
